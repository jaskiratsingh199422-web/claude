These must be confirmed and completed by Paytech's People & Culture and Benefits teams before this volume is treated as authoritative — the surrounding text describes the general structure and statutory minimums, not company-confirmed numbers.

1. Compensation & Pay
1.1 Pay Frequency & Method
Employees are paid by direct deposit on a [bi-weekly/semi-monthly — confirm] schedule. Pay stubs are available through [payroll system/portal name]. Any pay discrepancy should be reported to Payroll as soon as it is noticed, so it can be corrected in the next pay cycle wherever possible.
1.2 Overtime
Eligibility for overtime pay depends on an employee's role classification under the Ontario Employment Standards Act, 2000 (or the equivalent legislation in their province). Employees in eligible roles are paid overtime in accordance with statutory requirements; employees in exempt roles (typically managerial or supervisory positions, or those covered by specific ESA exemptions) are not. An employee's offer letter or role documentation confirms which classification applies to them.
1.3 Salary Reviews & Adjustments
Compensation is reviewed in connection with the Annual Review described in Section 6.2 of Volume 6, and may also be adjusted on promotion, role change, or as part of a broader compensation review cycle. Specific salary bands and increase guidelines are maintained by People & Culture and are not published company-wide, consistent with standard compensation confidentiality practice.
1.4 Incentive & Bonus Plans
Where a role is eligible for a bonus, commission, or other incentive plan, the structure, targets, and payout timing are set out in the employee's offer letter or a separate plan document, and are administered by People & Culture in partnership with Finance.
1.5 Deductions
Statutory deductions (income tax, CPP/QPP, EI) are withheld as required by law. Other deductions (e.g., benefits premiums, group RRSP contributions) are only made with the employee's authorization, as documented at enrollment.

3. Onboarding & Probation
3.1 Before Day One
New hires receive a welcome package covering start date, first-day logistics, IT equipment shipping/setup, and required paperwork (e.g., banking information for payroll, emergency contact details).
3.2 First Weeks
Onboarding typically includes an orientation session covering company policies, mandatory compliance training (see Section 5.2), introductions to the employee's team, and role-specific training led by the manager. Many teams also pair new hires with a peer buddy for their first weeks.
3.3 Probationary Period
New employees typically serve a probationary period of [length — e.g., 3 months, confirm] from their start date, during which performance and fit are actively assessed, consistent with the applicable employment standards legislation. Completion of probation does not change an employee's at-will or contractual status beyond what is set out in their employment agreement, and the general Termination provisions in Volume 6, Section 13, continue to apply throughout.
3.4 Equipment & Access
IT provisions equipment and system access in line with the employee's role, following the security obligations described in Volume 6, Section 4.3, for anyone working remotely.

4. Expenses & Business Travel
4.1 Reimbursable Expenses
Reasonable, pre-approved business expenses (e.g., approved travel, client meals within policy limits, approved home-office equipment) are reimbursed in accordance with the Expense Policy referenced in Volume 6, Section 4.5. Original receipts (or digital copies) are required for all claims above [threshold — confirm].
4.2 Submitting a Claim
Expenses are submitted through [expense system/portal], generally within [timeframe — e.g., 30 days, confirm] of being incurred, and require manager approval before reimbursement is processed by Payroll or Finance.
4.3 Business Travel
Business travel is booked through [travel booking process/provider], and should be arranged as far in advance as reasonably possible to manage cost. Travel outside the employee's home province or country may require additional approval — check with your manager and, where relevant, Compliance (for regulatory travel restrictions tied to certain roles).
4.4 Non-Reimbursable Items
Personal expenses, and any expense not genuinely required for business purposes, are not reimbursed. Where there is doubt about whether something qualifies, employees should check with their manager or Finance before incurring the cost.

5. Learning & Development
5.1 Mandatory Compliance Training
All employees are required to complete periodic training on topics tied directly to Volume 6, including Code of Conduct (Section 3), anti-money-laundering/anti-terrorist-financing obligations, privacy (Section 11), and harassment/violence prevention (Sections 8–9). Training is assigned and tracked through [LMS/training platform], and completion is mandatory within the assigned deadline — non-completion may itself be treated as a performance or conduct matter given its regulatory importance.
5.2 Professional Development
Employees are encouraged to pursue growth through coaching, internal training, conference attendance, or further education relevant to their role, consistent with the development focus described in Volume 6, Section 6.3. Where a tuition or certification reimbursement benefit applies, its terms (eligible programs, reimbursement cap, any service commitment) are set out in [Learning & Development policy / benefits summary].
5.3 Internal Mobility
Consistent with Volume 6, Section 12.4, employees are encouraged to apply for internal openings that support their career growth, generally after completing a minimum tenure in their current role (typically around 12 months, unless their manager and HR agree otherwise).

6. Workplace Health & Safety (General)
6.1 General Commitment
Beyond the workplace violence program described in Volume 6, Section 9, Paytech maintains a broader occupational health and safety program consistent with the Ontario Occupational Health and Safety Act and equivalent legislation elsewhere, covering general workplace safety, ergonomics, and incident response.
6.2 Reporting an Incident or Hazard
Any workplace injury, near-miss, or safety hazard — including ergonomic concerns from remote or in-office workstations — should be reported promptly to a manager and to [Health & Safety contact/HR]. Reports are logged and, where required by law, escalated to the relevant regulator.
6.3 Return-to-Work
Employees returning from a leave related to illness or injury are supported through a return-to-work process coordinated by HR, the employee's manager, and, where applicable, the disability insurer, focused on a safe and sustainable return, including modified duties where appropriate and medically supported.
6.4 Joint Health & Safety Committee
Where required by legislation, Paytech maintains a Joint Health & Safety Committee with employee and management representation. Details on current representatives and how to raise a concern with the Committee are available from [HR/Health & Safety contact].

7. Accommodation & Accessibility
7.1 Duty to Accommodate
Beyond the recruitment-stage accommodation described in Volume 6, Section 12.2, Paytech's duty to accommodate continues throughout employment for any protected ground under applicable human rights legislation — including disability, religious observance, and family status — up to the point of undue hardship.
7.2 Requesting an Accommodation
An employee who needs an accommodation (for example, a modified schedule, ergonomic equipment, or leave arrangement tied to a disability) should raise the request with their manager or HR. Supporting documentation may be requested only to the extent the law permits, and is handled confidentially, consistent with the Privacy principles in Volume 6, Section 11.
7.3 Accessibility Standards
Paytech is committed to meeting its obligations under the Accessible Canada Act and, in Ontario, the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA), including accessible customer service, information, and communication practices, and workplace emergency response plans for employees who need one.

8. Diversity, Equity & Inclusion
8.1 Commitment
Paytech is committed to building a workforce and workplace culture that reflects the diversity of the communities it serves, and to ensuring equitable access to opportunity regardless of any characteristic protected under applicable human rights law.
8.2 How This Shows Up
This commitment is reflected in the fair hiring practices described in Volume 6, Section 12.1, in how promotion and development opportunities are made available, and in Paytech's expectation — described in Volume 6, Section 3.2 — that every employee treats colleagues, clients, and partners with respect and fairness.
8.3 Employee Resource Groups
Where Paytech supports employee resource groups (ERGs) or similar voluntary employee communities, information on current groups and how to join is available from [HR/DEI contact].

9. Information Technology & Acceptable Use
9.1 Acceptable Use
Company systems, email, and devices are provided for legitimate business purposes. Limited, reasonable personal use is generally permitted provided it does not interfere with work, violate any policy in Volume 6 (particularly the Code of Conduct and Privacy sections), or create security or legal risk.
9.2 Account Security
Employees must use strong, unique passwords, enable multi-factor authentication where offered, and never share login credentials — consistent with the confidentiality expectations in Volume 6, Sections 3 and 11. Suspected account compromise should be reported immediately to IT Security.
9.3 Software & Devices
Only company-approved software may be installed on company devices. Personal devices used for work (where permitted) must meet the security requirements described in Volume 6, Section 4.3, before being used to access company systems.
9.4 Monitoring
Paytech may monitor use of its systems and devices to the extent necessary for security, compliance, and business purposes, consistent with applicable privacy law and the principles in Volume 6, Section 11. Employees should have no expectation of privacy in company systems used for company business.

11. General Workplace Concerns (Non-Harassment Grievances)
11.1 When to Use This Channel
Not every workplace concern is a harassment, violence, or code of conduct matter — an employee may simply disagree with a decision, have a concern about workload, team dynamics, or a process that isn't working well. This section describes how to raise that kind of concern.
11.2 How to Raise a Concern
Employees are encouraged to first raise the concern directly with their manager where that feels appropriate and safe to do. If that isn't appropriate, or the concern involves the manager themselves, the employee can raise it with HR directly, using the same multiple-route principle described in Volume 6, Section 3.4.
11.3 What Happens Next
HR will listen to the concern, ask clarifying questions, and work with the employee to identify a reasonable path forward — which may include a conversation facilitated by HR, a referral to another process (such as the Harassment or Discipline procedures) if the facts warrant it, or simply guidance on next steps. As with all HR matters, concerns are handled with discretion and shared only with those who need to know.
11.4 No Retaliation
Raising a good-faith workplace concern through this channel is protected in the same way as any other good-faith report described in Volume 6 — it will never be held against the employee raising it.
